The Beginner’s Zero-Knowledge Guide: How to Lock, Decrypt & Explore Vaults

A complete visual manual for anyone who wants absolute privacy. Learn how to turn private files into encrypted .zsafe archives (compatible with .zev) without touching the cloud or exposing passwords.

1. What Does "Zero-Knowledge" Actually Mean?

In conventional cloud storage or web-based encryptors, your files or password hashes are transmitted across a network to a remote server. Even services that advertise "end-to-end encryption" often rely on remote key databases or closed-source backends.

ZevSafe is fundamentally different: It runs entirely inside your browser's local sandbox using the W3C Web Cryptography API (crypto.subtle).

🛡️ The Zero-Knowledge Guarantee

Your files, passphrases, and decrypted contents never touch a server, disk, or remote API. You can disconnect your internet, turn on Airplane Mode, and ZevSafe will encrypt and decrypt 5 GB+ folders with zero network connectivity.

2. Step 1: Locking Your First Folder or Files

To lock files or a complete directory structure:

  1. Open the ZevSafe Portal on any computer, tablet, or smartphone.
  2. Select the Lock a Folder tab (or Lock Single File).
  3. Drag and drop your folder into the drop zone, or click Browse Folder to select your directory via the native operating system picker.
  4. ZevSafe reads your files directly in memory and shows a summary of total file count and uncompressed byte size.

3. Step 2: Choosing an Unbreakable Passphrase

Your encryption key is derived directly from your password using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 with a minimum of 600,000 iterations.

  • Manual Password: Type a master passphrase. The live entropy meter evaluates bit strength in real-time.
  • CSPRNG Diceware Generator: Click the 🎲 icon to automatically generate a 6-word Diceware passphrase (e.g. correct-horse-battery-staple-beacon-glory) with over 77 bits of cryptographic entropy.
💡 Pro Tip: Never Reuse Passwords

Because ZevSafe has no "Forgot Password" link and stores zero recovery data on any server, if you lose your password, your files are mathematically irrecoverable. Always store a backup!

4. Step 3: Generating the Paper-Safe Emergency Sheet

Before clicking encrypt, click the Generate Paper-Safe Recovery Sheet button. This generates a standalone, printable HTML cold-storage document containing:

  • The unique 32-byte CSPRNG salt fingerprint in formatted 8-character blocks.
  • Key derivation tier settings and container version indicators.
  • An encoded optical QR code that can be scanned by camera or clipboard to restore settings.
  • Handwriting boxes for your Diceware words so you can keep an offline physical backup in a safe.

5. Step 4: Unlocking & Exploring Your Vault

When encryption finishes, your browser downloads a single .zsafe file (e.g., Financial_Archive.zsafe). To inspect or extract its contents:

  1. Switch to the Unlock Vault screen in ZevSafe.
  2. Drag and drop your .zsafe (or legacy .zev) file into the unlock drop zone.
  3. Enter your master passphrase. If you configured a physical keyfile, attach it now.
  4. Click Unlock Vault. ZevSafe verifies the 57-byte header and decrypts the tail manifest in under 300 ms.
  5. The Web Vault Explorer opens, allowing you to browse directory trees, preview images in the lightbox, stream video without downloading, and extract individual files with 1-click.

6. Critical Rules for Safe Vault Management

  • The .zsafe File is Portable: You can store it on Google Drive, Dropbox, USB flash drives, or external SSDs. Because AES-256-GCM is military-grade, cloud providers cannot read the contents.
  • Safe Renaming: You can safely rename the .zsafe file on disk (e.g., from secret.zsafe to backup.zsafe) — the internal structure remains 100% intact.
  • Lock Session When Done: When you finish browsing in Vault Explorer, click the Lock Session button to immediately scrub encryption keys and zero out plaintext RAM.
